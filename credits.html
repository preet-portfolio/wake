credits & sources

wake is new work, written from scratch, but it stands on other people's work. This page lists everything it took inspiration, ideas or tools from.

uji by Noah Doersing is the direct inspiration. wake shares no code, presets, glyphs or layout with it; a token-level copy check against uji's original source found no shared code. The idea of a drawing as a line redrawn and nudged every step, with every step left on the page, came from playing with uji and reading its source.

Inspiration

uji — Noah Doersing, 2021
A minimalist generative art tool in the browser (live). The case study's early contact sheets and the 20-circle series were made with a modified fork of uji, which keeps its license. License: MIT, with a clause forbidding NFTs ("MIT except NFTs" v1.1).
Dōgen, Uji (“Being-Time”), 1240
A fascicle of the Shōbōgenzō, and the source of uji's name. Its idea that each moment is complete in itself shaped wake's per-point clocks and the series statement.

Lineage: drawing machines and plotter art

Vera Molnár, (Des)Ordres, 1974
Plotter drawings of ordered squares disturbed by controlled randomness. In wake this lineage shows up as jitter and gaps.
Desmond Paul Henry, drawing machines, from 1961
Machines built from analogue bombsight computers that left mechanical traces on paper. In wake, the drawing is the trace of motion.
Harmonograph — attributed to Hugh Blackburn, c. 1844
Pendulums summing periodic motions into a figure. These are wake's wave signals over time.
Spirograph — Denys Fisher, 1965
Rolling circles that produce epicycles: rotation about a moving origin.
Lissajous figures — Jules Antoine Lissajous, 1857
One of wake's seed shapes.
Ensō
The Zen circle drawn in one movement and often left open, which is what the plotter-tested series turned into.

Mathematics

Affine transforms and iterated maps
Each block is a small map applied every step; a wake is the orbit of a polyline under the stack.
Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula — Campbell 1897, Baker 1905, Hausdorff 1906
It explains a finding from the tests: for small steps, two transforms applied in either order differ only by a second-order term. That's why reordering wake's blocks changes little unless they're nonlinear.
Cosine palettes — Inigo Quilez
Every colour design's gradient is a + b·cos(2π(c·t + d)), from his article on procedural colour palettes. The twelve named palettes in wake are tuned from that formula.
mulberry32 — Tommy Ettinger
The seeded random number generator behind every random choice in wake, so the same link always gives the same drawing, and each of the 100,000 numbered designs is the same on every device. Public domain.
