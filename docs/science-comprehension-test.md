# 60-second comprehension test

**Question:** after one minute with a science piece and its ⓘ card, can a stranger say what they saw?

**Pass:** at least 3 of 5 people give a one-sentence explanation that contains the idea (column "idea" below), in their own words.

## Who
Five people who haven't seen wake, at least two without a science degree. Give each person one science piece (rotate through all six across the five sessions, plus one repeat).

| piece | the idea their sentence should contain |
|---|---|
| Lorenz attractor | tiny differences grow huge (chaos / butterfly effect) |
| double pendulum | the same start, give or take a hair, leads to completely different motion |
| figure eight | three bodies can share one orbit forever |
| fireflies | coupling past a threshold makes them suddenly sync |
| Chladni plate | sand gathers where the plate doesn't move |
| Turing patterns | spots and stripes can form by themselves from reacting chemicals |

## Setup
Open the piece's link (its ⓘ card opens with it). Say: **"Have a look for a minute. You can touch anything."** Start a timer and stay silent.

## At 60 seconds, ask
1. "In one sentence, what was happening?" Write it down word for word.
2. "Did you move the slider on the card? What changed?"
3. "Would you show this to someone? Who?"

## Record
| person | piece | their sentence | contains the idea? | used the slider? | would show someone? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Put results in `docs/science-comprehension-results.md`. A piece that fails gets its card sentence rewritten first, then is retested with someone new.
